# DROIDEX trademark and attribution policy

The DROIDEX source code is licensed under the [Apache License 2.0](LICENSE).
The DROIDEX **name, logo, icon, wordmark, and visual identity are not**. Section
6 of the license grants no rights to them, and this policy explains what that
means in practice.

The goal is simple: you are free to fork DROIDEX, change it, and ship it, as
long as nobody is confused about who made what.

## What the license already requires

If you redistribute DROIDEX or anything derived from it, in source or binary
form, Section 4 of the Apache License requires you to:

- include a copy of the `LICENSE` file;
- keep the copyright, patent, trademark, and attribution notices in the source
  you carry over;
- include the contents of the `NOTICE` file in your distribution, in your
  documentation or in the display your software produces; and
- **state prominently in every file you changed that you changed it.**

These are license obligations, not requests.

## What you must do when you fork

- **Say it is a fork.** State clearly and near the top of your README, website,
  and app description that your project is a fork or derivative of DROIDEX, and
  link back to https://github.com/droidex-anas/droid-maxxing. A single sentence
  is enough, for example: "Foo is a fork of [DROIDEX](https://github.com/droidex-anas/droid-maxxing)
  by Anas, licensed under Apache 2.0."
- **Use your own name and branding.** Rename the application, the binary, the
  bundle identifier, the update feed, and the icon. Do not ship a product called
  DROIDEX, or a name or logo close enough to be mistaken for it.
- **Do not imply endorsement.** Your fork is not official, supported, affiliated
  with, or approved by DROIDEX or its maintainer unless you have written
  permission.
- **Do not present the work as originally yours.** Removing the copyright,
  patent, trademark, or attribution notices the license requires you to keep is
  a license violation, and presenting the work as though you wrote it from
  scratch is what the fork disclosure above exists to prevent.

## What you may do without asking

- Say truthfully that your project is "based on DROIDEX", "a fork of DROIDEX",
  or "compatible with DROIDEX", in plain text and without styling the word as a
  logo.
- Use the DROIDEX name in articles, talks, reviews, tutorials, comparisons, and
  academic work.
- Keep the DROIDEX branding in an unmodified build you did not rename, for
  example when you mirror an official release without changing it.

## What needs written permission

- Using the DROIDEX name or logo as, or as part of, your own product, company,
  domain, app-store listing, or account name.
- Selling merchandise or services under the DROIDEX name.
- Any use suggesting that DROIDEX produced, sponsors, or endorses your work.

To ask, open a [discussion](https://github.com/droidex-anas/droid-maxxing/discussions) on the repository. Permission that is not given in
writing has not been given.

## Enforcement

Trademark obligations exist separately from the copyright license, so a fork can
comply with Apache 2.0 and still misuse the name. If a fork does not credit
DROIDEX or presents itself as the original, the maintainer may ask for it to be
corrected and may pursue the remedies the license and trademark law provide.
